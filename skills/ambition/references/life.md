# Life mode: finding the Ikigai

Run on `/ambition life`, "ikigai", or when a task is really a question about the user's direction. This runs the four lenses on the user's life itself and keeps the answer in `map.md`.

Ikigai is the overlap of four things: what you love, what you are good at, what you can be paid for, and what the world needs. Add a fifth ring that holds the rest in: the limits the user will not trade (time with family, health, location, an income floor, values).

## Principles

- **Context first, always.** Run step 1 of the task flow (`references/context.md`) before anything. Life mode without context is a horoscope. If the user skips context, offer the interview again once, then do a lighter version built on their answers to step 4 below.
- **Evidence before interview.** If the user has notes about themselves, read them before asking. Ask only to fill real gaps.
- **Shown beats stated.** What someone does when nobody asks is stronger evidence than what they say they want. Tag every claim `stated` or `shown`, with source and date.
- **The gaps are the gold.** Where stated and shown disagree, there is a hidden want or a story they tell themselves. Name each gap plainly and leave the judgement to them.
- **Concrete lives, not abstract values.** People choose between real Tuesdays far better than they rank "autonomy" against "impact".
- **Found by doing.** The map is a best guess. Cheap experiments move it. Thinking alone does not.
- **Never final, never the whole person.** Each version is dated, and old versions are kept. The map describes a direction, not an identity.

## Step 1. Gather the evidence (quietly)

From the context sources and profile, look for:
- **Direction:** north star, goals, future-self or values writing, reviews of past years.
- **Record:** CV, career notes, things shipped, results with numbers.
- **Market:** what employers, clients or customers have said or paid.
- `record.md` in full.

If the user has given access, look for shown evidence in places they did not write for this purpose:
- **What they make unasked:** commit history or projects worked on in evenings and weekends.
- **What they save and return to:** bookmarks, reading lists, recurring topics in past chats.
- **What others ask them for:** advice requests, repeat clients, invitations.
- **Energy:** any notes on which days or kinds of work felt good.

Respect house rules. Never guess a personal fact. Never reuse a claim the sources mark as false.

## Step 2. Draft the map

Use the template in `references/record.md`. For each of the four circles: three to six claims, each tagged `stated` or `shown`, with source and confidence. Then:
- **Overlaps:** what sits in two, three or all four circles.
- **Gaps:** where stated and shown disagree.
- **The ring:** the hard limits.
- **What the notes don't cover:** areas the evidence is silent on (play, friendships, creativity, place). Do not fill these with guesses. Ask, or leave them open.
- **Best guess, one sentence:** "<Name> does X, for Y, paid by Z, in a way that keeps W." With a confidence level.

## Step 3. Run the four lenses on the life

- **Speed:** the next obvious step they would take anyway.
- **Altitude:** a ladder from what they are doing now up to what it is all for. Is the current work aimed at the right rung, or a rung below?
- **Depth:** what the best career strategist would do. Rawest material: the actual calendar, energy notes, money and work record, not summaries. Instruments: a time-use audit, an energy-tagged week, real pay data for the roles in the overlap. Belief to test: the one belief most shaping their choices, tried against the evidence. Outside judge: the market's answer, their energy, a person who knows them.
- **Range:** three to five fields that solve "find work that fits a life", each with what it does. Examples: portfolio careerists, indie makers, athletes after their playing career, ecology's idea of a niche (where you thrive with least competition). One red team: a recruiter or sceptical buyer reading their story.
- **Outside the map:** one life that ignores the evidence entirely, built from the question "what if the notes are wrong about you?"
- **Unreasonable version:** the life where all four circles overlap at a much higher level, stated as a normal goal with a date.

Check every option against the ring.

## Step 4. Show three or four concrete lives

Build them from the overlaps, plus the outside-the-map one and the unreasonable one. Each is concrete: a Tuesday hour by hour, the weekly rhythm, the income and how it is earned, who they work with, where family fits, how their body is going.

Ask with AskUserQuestion, one round, at most three questions. Use option previews to show each Tuesday.
1. "Which of these Tuesdays pulls you most?"
2. "Which one would you hate?" (repulsion is as useful as pull)
3. Only if needed: the one gap the evidence cannot settle.

If an answer needs more than a pick, invite a few typed lines in "Other".

## Step 5. Pick experiments

Turn the pull into two or three experiments, each:
- cheap (under a week of effort, little or no money);
- short (about two weeks);
- with a signal to watch: energy after, whether someone asks for more, money offered, whether they did it again unasked.

Examples: sell one small piece of the overlap to one paying person; spend two mornings working as if in the chosen life and score the energy; publish one piece in the territory and see who replies.

Write each into `record.md` as a life bet with its signal and check date. If the user has a task system named in their context, offer to add them there.

## Step 6. Update and report

- Save the dated map (if they keep a data folder). Move the previous best guess under "Previous versions".
- Report: the best-guess sentence, the biggest gap, the chosen life, the experiments with dates. Short and plain.

## Tone

This is someone's life. Plain sentences only. No coaching clichés, no therapy words, nothing written for effect. If the evidence touches grief, separation, illness or money trouble, state facts from the sources and leave feelings to them. If they seem flat or tired, make the run smaller rather than pushing.
